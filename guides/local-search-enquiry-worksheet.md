# A practical worksheet for measuring local search enquiries

Local visibility is only useful when people can contact the business and the resulting enquiries fit what the business can actually provide. This worksheet separates discovery from contact and bookings so that a change in one number does not masquerade as a change in another.

## 1. Define a qualified enquiry

Before counting, write down the service, location and customer need that make an enquiry relevant. For example, a home-service company could count a request for a service it performs within its operating area, even if the caller is not ready to book. Record enquiries that do not meet the criteria separately; do not delete them from the total.

Review the definition with the person answering calls or forms. Apply the same rules in each reporting period.

## 2. Check that the contact path works

On a phone, open the relevant service page and tap the call button. Submit a permitted test through the form and confirm that it reaches the intended inbox or system. Label the test so it is not counted as a customer enquiry. Check the business profile and website for consistent, current contact details. Record the date, device, page and result of each check.

## 3. Keep a small weekly log

| Week | Discovery indicators | Calls and forms | Qualified enquiries | Bookings | Unknown source | Changes or issues |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Enter the week | Record the source and measure used | Exclude labelled tests and duplicates | Apply the definition above | Count confirmed bookings | Count enquiries with no reliable source | Note outages, edits and holidays |

Discovery indicators can include search impressions, clicks or profile interactions. These are different measures from people, enquiries and bookings; do not add them together. A click-to-call event is not proof that a call connected, and a connected call is not automatically a qualified lead.

## 4. Compare like with like

Compare equal periods using the same definitions. If enquiries fall while discovery stays steady, check contact paths, business hours and how leads are handled before assuming a ranking change. If discovery increases but qualified enquiries do not, review whether the search queries, pages and services align with actual customer needs. Small counts can fluctuate sharply, so inspect individual changes before drawing a conclusion.

## 5. Treat attribution as evidence, not certainty

Ask customers how they found the business when practical, but leave the source unknown when the answer or tracking data is inconclusive. Avoid storing sensitive call or form details in a shared worksheet. Aggregate counts are usually sufficient for a weekly review.

For the distinction between discovery, enquiries and bookings, see [Is local search bringing the right enquiries?](https://seanguillermo.mataroa.blog/blog/is-local-search-bringing-the-right-enquiries/). Google's guidance on [establishing business details](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/establish-business-details) provides a useful reference for keeping information discoverable and consistent.
